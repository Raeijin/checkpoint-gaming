Quick answer

For console racing, choose the T598 version licensed for your console: Xbox/PC and PlayStation/PC are separate products. For PC, compare the delivered bundle price, wheel ecosystem and pedal plans. The R5 is a compact direct-drive option; its 5.5 Nm peak figure does not prove it is stronger than the T598’s 5 Nm constant rating.

BUY IF
  • You are moving from a gear- or belt-driven wheel to entry direct drive
  • You have checked which platform version you actually need
  • You want a complete bundle rather than a wheelbase-only comparison
SKIP IF
  • You are treating the torque number as the whole buying decision
  • You need console support but are looking at the PC-focused MOZA R5

MOZA R5 vs Thrustmaster T598: the short answer

If you race mainly on PC, shortlist the R5 when the complete bundle price is competitive. It is compact, established, includes a desk clamp and gives you a clear MOZA ecosystem path. The T598 becomes more compelling when console licensing matters, because Thrustmaster sells dedicated Xbox/PC and PlayStation/PC versions.

Force feedback: 5.5 Nm versus 5 Nm is not a knockout

MOZA rates the R5 at 5.5 Nm peak torque. Thrustmaster rates the T598 at 5 Nm of constant torque and advertises short overshoot capability for stronger transient effects. Those numbers are measured and marketed differently, so this is not a clean “5.5 beats 5” comparison.

Both sit in the useful entry direct-drive range where the bigger upgrade over a Logitech G923-class wheel is the direct connection between motor and wheel rather than a tiny torque difference between these two.

Platform support is the first real decision

The standard MOZA R5 bundle is a PC product. The T598 is sold in separate licensed versions: one for Xbox One / Xbox Series X|S / PC and another for PS4 / PS5 / PC. If you race on console, this can decide the comparison before you get to pedals, wheel rims or software.

Pedals: both start with two pedals

The R5 bundle uses MOZA's SR-P Lite throttle and brake. The T598 bundle includes Thrustmaster Raceline Pedals LTE, also as a two-pedal starter set. Neither should be bought under the assumption that a clutch is automatically included.

For serious braking consistency later, a load-cell upgrade is more meaningful than arguing over the starter pedal faceplates.

Wheel and ecosystem

MOZA uses its quick-release ecosystem with a broad range of wheels, pedals, shifters and handbrakes. Thrustmaster's T598 uses a detachable 30 cm Sportcar wheel and opens the door to Thrustmaster wheel add-ons, although some older rims can require an adapter or firmware update.

Desk use

MOZA explicitly sells the R5 as suitable for limited-space desk setups and includes the table clamp. The T598 bundle also includes a clamping system. In both cases, desk stiffness matters: a flexible desktop can waste detail and make the whole setup feel worse.

T598 Race Dash is a genuine differentiator

The T598 has a built-in screen for settings and compatible telemetry information. That is something the R5 does not have on the wheelbase itself. Whether it matters depends on how much you value on-device adjustment versus doing configuration through MOZA Pit House on PC.

Which one would we buy?

PC only: start with the MOZA R5 and compare the live complete-bundle price. Xbox: the T598 Xbox/PC version has the obvious platform advantage. PlayStation: the T598 PlayStation/PC version is the relevant product. If both are PC options at similar money, ecosystem preference and pedal upgrade plans matter more than the half-newton-metre headline.

Is this the R5 or the R5 Pro comparison?

This page covers the original R5. If your local retailer is offering the newer Pro, use our R5 Pro vs T598 comparison and R5 vs R5 Pro breakdown instead. Keeping the exact bundle name on your shortlist prevents comparing one model’s price with another model’s specifications.

What to budget after the bundle

Check whether you need a clutch, a compatible wheel rim or a stand. Neither standard two-pedal set should be treated as a load-cell brake upgrade. If you already own Thrustmaster add-ons, check the official compatibility list and any required adapter before counting them as a saving.

COMMON QUESTIONS

Frequently asked questions

Does the T598 work on Xbox and PlayStation?

There are separate Xbox/PC and PlayStation/PC versions. One console version does not automatically support the other console.

Is the R5 stronger than the T598?

The R5 specification is 5.5 Nm peak; the T598 is advertised at 5 Nm constant with transient overshoot. These are different ratings, so the numbers alone do not establish a winner.
