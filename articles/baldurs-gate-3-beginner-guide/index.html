BALDUR'S GATE 3 GUIDE

Baldur's Gate 3 Beginner Guide: The Systems Worth Understanding First

A practical introduction to actions, bonus actions, rests, concentration, advantage and why talking can be stronger than fighting.

Checked 20 August 2026•10 MIN•Research-backed
ON THIS PAGE Quick answer Key habits Main guide What to buy / use Related articles
Quick answer

Learn the action economy first: each turn is built around movement, an action and often a bonus action. Use high ground and advantage, protect concentration spells, rest regularly, and save before decisions you genuinely care about.

DO THIS
  • Talk, inspect and experiment instead of rushing combat
  • Use short and long rests deliberately
  • Build around party roles rather than four copies of the same job
AVOID THIS
  • Reload every imperfect dice roll
  • Hoard consumables forever
  • Assume every encounter has one correct solution
AT A GLANCE

Key facts

Understand actions and bonus actions

Most characters get one main action per turn plus movement, and many builds have useful bonus actions. A turn becomes much stronger when you deliberately use both rather than ending after a single attack.

Advantage is a huge deal

Attacking from favourable positions, hiding, restraining enemies or using class abilities can give advantage. It improves reliability and is often more valuable than chasing a tiny damage increase.

Concentration changes spell choices

Many powerful spells require concentration, and a character normally maintains only one concentration spell at a time. Casting another concentration spell replaces the first.

Rest more often than you think

Short and long rests restore important resources. The game also uses camp conversations to advance companion stories, so avoiding rests indefinitely can mean missing character moments.

Talking is gameplay

Dialogue skills, disguises, spells and class-specific choices can resolve encounters or create completely different outcomes. Combat is only one solution.

Action economy beats flashy individual attacks

A turn where the party controls space, creates advantage and focuses one dangerous target can be stronger than four unrelated damage buttons. Look for ways to combine shove, surfaces, spells, high ground and concentration rather than treating each character in isolation.

Talk to people before drawing weapons

Dialogue can reveal alternate routes, allies, merchants and information that combat cannot. Skills such as Persuasion, Deception, Intimidation and Insight create options, but failure can be interesting too—do not feel forced to reload every imperfect roll.

Rest before the party becomes useless

Short rests and long rests are resources, but hoarding them can make the game harder than necessary. Many companion scenes also occur at camp, so resting is part of story progression as well as resource recovery.

Concentration is a hidden beginner trap

Many powerful spells require concentration, and casting another concentration spell can end the first one. Read the spell card before stacking effects you cannot actually maintain. Protecting the character who holds an important concentration spell can be more valuable than squeezing out one extra attack.

Consumables are part of the combat system

Potions, scrolls, arrows, elixirs and throwable items are abundant enough to use. A scroll can let a character solve a problem outside their normal spell list, while a potion thrown at the ground can sometimes help more than one nearby ally. Hoarding everything until the credits means you paid the inventory-management tax without getting the benefit.

Use difficulty settings deliberately

If you enjoy the role-playing but combat is blocking progress, lowering difficulty is a valid tool. If encounters feel trivial, raise it. The best setting is the one that keeps choices interesting rather than turning every fight into either a wall or a formality.
