MOZA R3 vs R5: Is 3.9 Nm Enough or Should You Start at 5.5 Nm?

The two obvious entry points into MOZA direct drive compared for PC and Xbox buyers.

Checked 20 August 2026•10 MIN•Research-backed
ON THIS PAGE Quick answer Who it suits Key specs Main guide What to buy / use Related articlesSources
Quick answer

Choose the R3 when price, Xbox compatibility or a very compact first setup matters most. Choose the R5 when you are on PC and want noticeably more torque headroom without jumping to a larger mid-range base.

BUY IF
  • You want the cheapest route into MOZA direct drive
  • Xbox compatibility is essential and you choose the licensed R3 bundle
  • Desk space and lower peak force matter
SKIP IF
  • You are on PC and can afford the R5 without compromising pedals or mounting
  • You know you will want more torque quickly
  • You are buying a base without checking platform compatibility

The important difference

The R3 is rated at 3.9 Nm peak torque, while the standard R5 is rated at 5.5 Nm. Both are direct-drive systems, so even the R3 avoids the gear-driven feel associated with older entry-level wheels.

Platform support

MOZA sells the R3 in both PC-only and officially licensed Xbox-and-PC versions. The standard R5 bundle is positioned as a PC product, so Xbox buyers should not assume the R5 is a drop-in upgrade.

Who should buy the R3?

It makes sense for a first desk-mounted setup, especially if you are unsure how deep you will go into sim racing. The bundle includes the base, wheel, two-pedal set and table clamp.

Who should buy the R5?

The extra torque gives more headroom for stronger steering forces and for retaining detail when force feedback is turned up. It is the better starting point for a PC buyer who already knows sim racing will be a long-term hobby.

Verdict

The R3 is the value-first entry point. The R5 is the stronger long-term PC buy when the budget allows.

How much does 3.9 Nm versus 5.5 Nm matter?

The R3 still gives you the direct connection and smooth response that makes modern entry direct drive appealing. The R5 simply has more room for heavier steering weight and sharp effects before the base hits its torque ceiling. For casual driving, the R3 can be enough; for a PC user who already knows they want to grow the rig, the R5 is easier to keep for longer.

Do not ignore the platform version

The licensed Xbox R3 exists because console compatibility is not just a USB question. If Xbox is part of the plan, buy the exact supported bundle. PC buyers have more freedom and should compare total price, included clamp and future pedals.

What to spend the saved money on

If choosing the R3 leaves enough budget for a stable stand, better seating or a later load-cell pedal upgrade, that can be smarter than stretching to the R5 while the rest of the setup moves around.
