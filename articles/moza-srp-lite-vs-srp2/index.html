MOZA SR-P Lite vs SRP2: Keep the R5 Pedals or Upgrade?

For original R5 owners: SR-P Lite brake kit or SRP2 replacement? Compare sensing, PC connections, mounting and the parts to check before buying.

Quick answer

Keep SR-P Lite if your budget or mounting is the limiting factor. Choose SRP2 when you specifically want a load-cell brake and can keep the pedals and seat stable. The original SR-P Lite performance kit changes resistance; it is not a load-cell conversion. This comparison concerns the original Lite, not Lite2.

BUY IF
  • You own the original R5 pedals and want a specific brake upgrade
  • You can secure the pedal deck and chair
  • Your PC game accepts separate wheel and pedal USB devices
SKIP IF
  • You are confusing SR-P, SR-P Lite, SR-P Lite2 and SRP2
  • The pedals slide every time you brake
  • You expect a new sensor to fix technique without practice

Start with what is actually wrong with your brake

If you cannot repeat a braking point, identify whether the pedal slides, your chair rolls back, or the input feels difficult to judge. A new pedal set only addresses some of those problems. Mark the pedal and chair positions with tape, drive a short session, then check whether either has moved. Fix movement before choosing a stiffer brake.

This is a researched buying comparison, not a hands-on lap-time test. Our recommendation is based on the connection and sensing differences below, plus the cost of making the whole setup usable.

Four similar names, different purchases

The original R5 includes SR-P Lite. The R5 Pro includes SR-P Lite2. SR-P and SRP2 are separate product names, so an accessory labelled for one should not be assumed to fit another. Read the name on your current set before ordering a clutch, spring kit or replacement.

MOZA’s original R5 vs R5 Pro bundles are different purchase decisions from replacing pedals you already own. This page helps original Lite owners decide whether to keep or replace them.

What the brake kit changes

The official original Lite performance kit uses a spring and damping block to change resistance. The pedal still senses travel through its existing sensor. A firmer stopping point may be what you want, but buying that kit is not the same as buying pressure sensing. Check MOZA’s compatibility table in Sources rather than assuming the SR-P accessory fits the Lite.

Keeping the current pedals is reasonable if you are still learning, have a flexible desk setup or would need to sacrifice a secure mount to fund the replacement. Add up the delivered kit price and any mounting work; stop if those costs get too close to the replacement you actually want.

What SRP2 changes

SRP2 combines a load cell with an angle sensor. It gives you a different way to configure the brake input, rather than simply adding a stronger spring to the original pedal. The sensor’s 100 kg rating is not a requirement to press with that force at your foot. Set a comfortable, repeatable maximum and recalibrate after changing resistance.

Connection checklist for an R5 owner on PC

  1. Keep the wheel connected normally and plan a separate PC USB connection for SRP2.
  2. Confirm your chosen game accepts inputs from separate wheel and pedal devices.
  3. Follow the pedal manual, configure the pedals in Pit House and assign the throttle and brake axes in the game.
  4. Remove duplicate brake assignments and check zero and full input before entering a race.

Do not infer console support from this USB route. This article’s recommendation is for PC; a console purchase needs confirmation of the complete licensed wheel, base and pedal combination.

Budget for the mount, not just the pedals

Check the mounting drawing against your pedal tray before paying. Include bolts or a suitable adapter plate, and make sure the seat stays put under your chosen braking force. You do not need the hardest setting to benefit from a repeatable setup. If a purchase leaves no money to stop the rig moving, postpone it.

Which route should you choose?

Keep Lite: you need an inexpensive resistance change or are still sorting out your driving position. Buy SRP2: you have stable mounting and specifically want load-cell braking. Consider CRP2: you have already decided to replace the pedals and want to compare a more adjustable alternative; use our SRP2 vs CRP2 comparison for that separate decision.

For shifters, wheel rims and the order of other purchases, return to the general R5 upgrade guide. There is no need to buy those accessories to make this pedal change worthwhile.
