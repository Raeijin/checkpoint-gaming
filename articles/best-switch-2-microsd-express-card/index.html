Best microSD Express Cards for Nintendo Switch 2: What to Buy and What Not to Reuse

Switch 2 requires microSD Express for game storage. Samsung P9 Express and SanDisk’s licensed cards are the straightforward branded options to compare.

Quick answer

Nintendo Switch 2 requires microSD Express cards for expandable game storage; an old standard microSD/microSDXC card from the original Switch will not work for this job. We would compare Samsung P9 Express and SanDisk microSD Express options, then choose capacity and price rather than paying extra for decorative branding.

BUY IF
  • Your Switch 2 internal storage is filling with digital games
  • The card explicitly says microSD Express
  • You want 256GB–512GB extra space now rather than constantly redownloading
SKIP IF
  • The listing says only microSDXC/UHS-I and not microSD Express
  • You are buying an old original-Switch card because it is cheaper
  • You have not updated the Switch 2 before first card use

The compatibility rule is simple—and easy to get wrong

Nintendo says Switch 2 is only compatible with microSD Express cards for downloadable software, updates, DLC, screenshots and captured videos. A normal microSD card from an original Switch cannot be used to store Switch 2 games.

The label matters. 'microSDXC', 'UHS-I' or a familiar Nintendo character printed on an older card does not automatically mean Express.

Samsung P9 Express

Samsung's P9 Express is explicitly positioned for Nintendo Switch 2 and uses PCIe-based microSD Express technology. Samsung claims sequential reads up to 800 MB/s and sells 256GB and 512GB capacities, with thermal management and a three-year limited warranty.

SanDisk microSD Express for Nintendo Switch 2

SanDisk offers both general microSD Express cards and officially licensed Switch 2 versions. Its current licensed data sheet lists read speeds up to 880 MB/s and write speeds up to 650 MB/s, with capacities up to 512GB and a lifetime limited warranty.

Do you need the officially licensed artwork?

No. Nintendo says any card up to 2TB that meets the microSD Express standard can be used. Licensed branding can be reassuring and convenient, but the technical standard is what matters.

How much capacity?

256GB is a sensible cheaper expansion for a mostly physical-game library. 512GB is easier to recommend for digital-heavy players because it gives more room before storage management returns. Larger Express capacities can make sense later as pricing improves.

Important first-use step

Nintendo requires a system update via the internet the first time a microSD Express card is used. If a new card is not detected, update the console before assuming the card is faulty.

Verdict

Buy a known-brand microSD Express card, not merely a fast-looking microSD card. Compare Samsung P9 Express and SanDisk Express pricing in the capacity you need, and let price, warranty and availability decide it.

How to avoid buying the wrong card

Search results can place older UHS-I cards beside microSD Express products. For Switch 2 game storage, verify the listing explicitly says microSD Express; do not rely on capacity, Nintendo artwork or the fact that the card worked in an original Switch. Buy capacity after compatibility, not before it.
