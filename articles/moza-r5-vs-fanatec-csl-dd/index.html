MOZA R5 vs Fanatec CSL DD 5 Nm: Which Beginner Direct-Drive Bundle Is Better?

Two of the most obvious first direct-drive setups compared on torque, pedals, ecosystem, upgrade path and platform support.

Checked 20 August 2026•12 MIN•Research-backed

Quick answer

For a straightforward PC setup, the MOZA R5 bundle is the simpler all-in-one package: 5.5 Nm direct drive, wheel, pedals and table clamp are included. Fanatec's CSL DD 5 Nm bundle starts at similar territory but has the stronger torque upgrade path to 8 Nm and wider console possibilities depending on the wheel used.

BUY IF
  • You want an entry direct-drive system rather than gears or belts
  • You are building a PC-first rig
  • You care about a clear upgrade path
SKIP IF
  • You need PlayStation support from this exact hardware
  • Your desk or cockpit cannot hold a direct-drive base securely
  • You are choosing purely by torque number rather than ecosystem

The short version

Both bundles are a major step above gear-driven entry-level wheels because the wheel is driven directly by the motor rather than through gears or belts.

MOZA R5

MOZA specifies 5.5 Nm peak torque, a 1000Hz USB refresh rate and 2700° maximum steering angle. The bundle includes the R5 wheelbase, ES steering wheel, SR-P Lite two-pedal set and a table clamp. The platform listed by MOZA is PC.

Fanatec CSL DD 5 Nm

Fanatec's current Ready2Race bundle includes the CSL DD QR2 base, CSL Pedals and P1 V2 wheel. The base produces 5 Nm peak torque and can be upgraded to 8 Nm with Fanatec's Boost Kit 180. PC is supported; Xbox support depends on using an Xbox-licensed Fanatec steering wheel. This particular CSL DD base cannot be made PlayStation compatible.

Which should you buy?

Choose the R5 when you want a neat PC-only starter package with the desk clamp already included. Choose the CSL DD when the 8 Nm upgrade path and Fanatec ecosystem matter more to you, or when potential Xbox compatibility is useful.

What about the pedals?

Neither starter pedal set should be the end of the journey if you get serious. Pedal upgrades—especially a load-cell brake—can improve consistency more than adding a few extra Newton metres of wheel torque.

Ecosystem matters more than the first-day spec sheet

The wheelbase is only the start. Wheels, pedals, shifters, handbrakes, dashboards and console licensing can lock you into an ecosystem for years. MOZA's appeal is a compact PC-first range with a straightforward bundle and lots of add-ons. Fanatec's appeal is a long-established modular ecosystem and the simple 5 Nm-to-8 Nm power-supply upgrade on the CSL DD.

Desk mounting versus cockpit mounting

At this torque level, a solid desk can work, but flex changes how the force feedback feels. The R5 bundle including a clamp is convenient. With Fanatec, make sure the mount you plan to use is included or budgeted separately. Whichever route you take, stabilising the pedals often matters more than increasing wheel torque.

Value rule

Compare the complete working setup: base, wheel, pedals, clamp or mount, shipping and the first upgrade you already know you want. A bundle that looks cheaper can stop being cheaper once the missing mount or pedal option is added.
