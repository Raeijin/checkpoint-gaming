MOZA SRP2 vs CRP2: Which Load-Cell Pedals Make More Sense?

MOZA's SRP2 and higher-end CRP2 compared on load-cell range, materials, adjustability and who should actually spend more.

Checked 20 August 2026•12 MIN•Research-backed

Quick answer

For most first serious pedal upgrades, the SRP2 is the value pick. The CRP2 is for a more serious rigid rig where its premium construction and deeper tuning range will actually be used.

BUY IF
  • You want a first serious load-cell-style pedal upgrade
  • Your cockpit can resist harder braking forces
  • You value repeatable braking more than stronger wheel torque
SKIP IF
  • Your pedals are not securely mounted
  • You are expecting pedals alone to fix driving technique
  • The CRP2 premium would force you to compromise the rest of the rig

Which makes more sense?

The SRP2 is the easier first serious pedal upgrade for most buyers. The CRP2 is aimed at rigs where premium construction and deeper adjustment are worth paying for.

Who should buy SRP2?

Buy it when you want a meaningful braking upgrade without immediately jumping to flagship-pedal pricing.

Who should buy CRP2?

Buy it when the rest of your cockpit is already strong enough to take advantage of a higher-end pedal set.

The decision is really about the rest of the rig

Pedals translate leg force into braking input, so mounting rigidity matters enormously. A premium pedal set on a sliding floor plate is wasted hardware. Before choosing CRP2 over SRP2, make sure the seat, pedal deck and driving position can handle harder and more repeatable braking.

Why braking upgrades can beat wheel upgrades

More wheel torque makes the car feel stronger, but a better brake can directly improve repeatability into corners. Learning to hit a similar pressure point lap after lap is one of the clearest reasons serious sim racers move beyond light position-based starter brakes.

SRP2 or CRP2?

Start with SRP2 when you want the functional jump without paying for maximum adjustability. Move to CRP2 when you already know you care about pedal geometry, feel tuning and a more premium mechanical platform—and you have the cockpit to use it properly.

Adjustment only matters if you use it

Premium pedals earn their price through mechanical range: pedal angle, spacing, resistance, travel and brake feel can be tuned around the driver's body and cockpit. If you prefer to bolt the pedals down once and never touch them again, some of the CRP2's value proposition disappears.

Think about footwear and driving position

A very stiff brake that feels excellent in shoes can be uncomfortable in socks, and a pedal angle that works in a formula seating position can feel wrong at a desk. Set the pedal decision around how you actually drive rather than copying someone else's resistance settings.

Bottom line

SRP2 is the pragmatic performance upgrade. CRP2 is the enthusiast choice for a driver who already has a rigid cockpit and wants to tune pedal feel as carefully as wheelbase settings.
