MOZA R5 vs R5 Pro: Which One Is Worth Buying?

R5 or R5 Pro? Compare the original 5.5 Nm bundle with the 6 Nm Pro, included pedals and the upgrade cost for new buyers and existing R5 owners.

Quick answer

For a new PC buyer, favour the R5 Pro when its delivered price is close to the R5 and its delivery date works for you. Keep an existing R5 unless you have a specific reason to replace it: the small torque step alone is hard to justify. A discounted R5 can leave more budget for stable mounting or pedals.

BUY IF
  • The R5 Pro price is close to the older R5
  • You want the newer encoder and feedback platform
  • You are buying your first complete PC direct-drive bundle
SKIP IF
  • The standard R5 is heavily discounted
  • You already own an R5 and expect a dramatic upgrade from 5.5 to 6 Nm
  • You really need 9 Nm+ headroom instead

R5 versus R5 Pro in one sentence

The R5 Pro is an evolutionary update rather than a completely different class of wheelbase.

Torque

The standard R5 is rated at 5.5 Nm peak torque. The R5 Pro increases that to 6 Nm. The numerical increase is small; it is not evidence of a dramatic improvement in driving performance.

Encoder and feedback system

MOZA lists the standard R5 with a 15-bit encoder and NexGen 4.0 force feedback. The R5 Pro moves to a 21-bit magnetic encoder and NexGen 5.0.

Bundle contents

The R5 Pro bundle includes the compact 6 Nm base, ES Lite steering wheel and SR-P Lite2 pedals. It remains aimed at buyers who want a complete starter system rather than building a wheelbase, wheel and pedals separately.

Verdict

If the R5 is heavily discounted, it can still be the value choice. At similar delivered pricing, the R5 Pro is our preference for a new buyer, provided the quoted dispatch date suits you.

Is 0.5 Nm worth upgrading for?

Not by itself. An existing R5 owner should not expect the move from 5.5 to 6 Nm to transform the experience. The R5 Pro is more compelling as a new purchase because the encoder and force-feedback generation also move forward.

Who should keep buying the older R5?

A meaningful clearance discount can still make the R5 excellent value. If the saving pays for a firmer wheel stand or a better brake pedal, the older bundle may produce the stronger overall setup.

Who should skip both?

If you already know you want a rigid cockpit and stronger steering, compare the R9 class before spending repeatedly on small torque steps. The sweet spot is not the same for a first-time desk racer and someone building a permanent rig.

New buyer versus existing R5 owner

The R5 Pro is easier to justify for a new buyer than as an upgrade from an existing R5. An owner already at 5.5 Nm will usually get more from pedals, mounting or a different wheel than replacing the entire bundle for a 0.5 Nm torque increase. New buyers should compare live bundle pricing and take the Pro when the gap is modest.

SR-P Lite2 is not SRP2

The Pro bundle includes SR-P Lite2 Hall-sensor pedals. Do not mistake the similar name for the separate SRP2 load-cell set. A newer bundle does not automatically give you pressure-based braking.

Check delivery as well as the price gap

A modest price premium is less useful if the newer bundle arrives too late for you. Compare the product-specific dispatch estimate, delivery destination and return terms on both listings. We do not treat an order button as a promise of immediate stock.

Where should an R5 owner spend next?

If the wheel is doing what you need, use the R5 upgrade priority guide to identify the weak point. For the specific original-pedal replacement decision, see SR-P Lite vs SRP2. Spend on a problem you can describe rather than replacing a working bundle to gain half a newton metre.
