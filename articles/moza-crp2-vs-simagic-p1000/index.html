MOZA CRP2 vs Simagic P1000: Which Pedals Fit Your PC Rig?

CRP2 or P1000? Compare complete pedal configurations, PC compatibility, mounting and optional upgrades before paying for a load-cell set.

Quick answer

Choose CRP2 if its delivered configuration suits you and you prefer configuring pedals in MOZA Pit House. Choose P1000 if you specifically want its modular hydraulic or haptic upgrade path. On PC, either can work alongside another brand of wheel through USB. Compare equal pedal counts and accessories before deciding which is better value.

BUY IF
  • You have a rigid pedal mount and are choosing a complete load-cell set
  • You have priced the actual two- or three-pedal configuration you need
  • You can identify an adjustment or accessory you will use
SKIP IF
  • You are choosing solely by the load-cell capacity number
  • You expect hydraulic or haptic accessories in every P1000 box
  • You need native console compatibility without checking the complete system

Compare two complete shopping baskets

These are alternatives for a PC racer who already wants a substantial pedal replacement. The useful comparison is the set you will bolt into your rig, not the lowest price shown beside each family name. Decide whether you use a clutch, whether you want an inverted layout and whether feedback accessories are part of the purchase now or only a future possibility.

We have not tested these sets back to back. The judgments here concern specifications, installation and purchase choices; they are not claims that one makes you faster.

Why 200 kg versus 100 kg does not decide the winner

MOZA lists a 200 kg load cell for CRP2; SIMAGIC lists 100 kg for P1000. Those are sensor ratings, not directly comparable force requirements at the pedal face. Linkage geometry, your chosen resistance and calibration affect the experience. Do not double your budget merely because a sensor number doubles.

A better question is whether you can reach and release your chosen braking input comfortably while the seat and pedal deck remain still. If you cannot, the setup needs attention before a higher sensor rating becomes useful.

Clutch and variant traps

The CRP2 listing used here is throttle plus brake with an optional clutch. SIMAGIC distinguishes the standard P1000, two-pedal P1000-F, inverted variants and hydraulic variants. Use the official variant table in Sources, then match the retailer’s full model name and box contents to it.

For paddle-shift GT or formula driving, a clutch may not justify its cost or footprint. For manual cars, compare both baskets with a clutch added where necessary. Do not treat a cheaper two-pedal listing as a like-for-like discount on a three-pedal set.

PC compatibility does not lock you to a wheel brand

Both manufacturers document direct PC USB connections. A MOZA wheelbase alone is therefore not a reason to rule out SIMAGIC pedals, and a SIMAGIC base is not required for this PC route. Confirm your game supports separate input devices, calibrate in the relevant software and assign pedal axes in the game.

This does not establish native Xbox or PlayStation compatibility. Console users need confirmation of the complete supported connection chain before ordering. Do not buy a PC USB pedal set on the assumption that a console will recognize it independently.

When the P1000 upgrade path is worth paying for

SIMAGIC documents optional haptic reactors, hydraulic modules and inversion options, with accessory power requirements to check. Make a list of the exact modules you want and price the complete package, including power and mounting. A future upgrade path is useful only if you will use it.

Hydraulic damping changes pedal feel; haptic feedback conveys effects. Neither is a substitute for a comfortable brake position. If you want one specific effect, establish software and game support before buying the module. Start with the base configuration and learn it before adding several variables at once.

When CRP2 makes more sense

CRP2 is worth shortlisting when you prefer the MOZA configuration workflow and its available mechanical adjustment fits your seating position. Its clutch and inversion kit are separate purchases. MOZA also lists a pedal haptic kit, so do not assume SIMAGIC is the only route to pedal vibration; verify the exact compatible kit and total cost for either brand.

If your real question is whether you need to spend this much at all, the SRP2 vs CRP2 guide covers MOZA’s less expensive alternative. That is a different decision from choosing between these two pedal families.

Measure before ordering

  1. Compare the manufacturer’s mounting drawing with your pedal tray’s holes and slots.
  2. Allow room for the control box, cables, pedal travel and any inversion frame.
  3. Check foot reach from your normal seated position, not while leaning forward.
  4. Include shipping, tax, a clutch if required and accessories in both totals.
  5. Check the seller’s return process and local warranty support before choosing an overseas deal.

Our buying decision

For a straightforward replacement, buy the configuration that fits your rig and offers the better delivered value. Prefer P1000 when you have a specific plan for its modular options; prefer CRP2 when its adjustment and MOZA software fit your needs. If both solve the same problem, local support and the complete cost can be more valuable than another unused feature.
